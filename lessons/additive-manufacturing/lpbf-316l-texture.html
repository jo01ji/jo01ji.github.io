    How the texture forms

    1 · Epitaxy and ⟨100⟩ growth

    316L solidifies as fcc austenite (sometimes with a little δ-ferrite). Cells and dendrites in cubic metals grow fastest along ⟨100⟩. A new pool does not nucleate fresh grains; it continues the grains at its fusion boundary, and each cell picks the ⟨100⟩ of that grain closest to the local thermal gradient, which points roughly normal to the pool boundary.

    2 · Melt pool shape sets the cell directions

    At the bottom of the pool the gradient is vertical, so cells grow along BD. On the side walls the gradient leans toward the pool centre. In a near-parabolic conduction pool much of the wall sits near 45°, giving ±45° cells. A grain that can feed both +45° and −45° cells with two of its ⟨100⟩ axes has ⟨110⟩ along BD and its third ⟨100⟩ along the scan direction. Steep keyhole walls instead give near-horizontal cells, which ⟨100⟩∥BD grains can serve.

    3 · Hatch and layer decide what survives

    Every pool is partly remelted by the next track (overlap = 1 − h/W) and by the next layer (remelt depth ≈ D − t). Only the surviving part of each pool is the substrate for the next one. Tight hatch spacing removes most side-wall material and leaves the vertical-cell centre, favouring ⟨100⟩. Wide hatch leaves more side wall, favouring ⟨110⟩, until gaps open and you get lack-of-fusion pores.

    4 · Rotation decides whether in-plane order survives

    With 0° rotation the scan direction never changes, so the selected grains keep one in-plane orientation and the part becomes single-crystal-like. With 90° rotation a grain must have ⟨100⟩ along both X and Y, which only the cube orientation offers. Any rotation that is not a multiple of 90°, such as 67°, keeps changing the required in-plane direction, so grains align only about BD: a fibre texture, usually weaker.

    5 · Speed and stray grains

    Higher speed shrinks the pool, reduces remelting and raises the chance of grains that do not grow epitaxially (stray grains, partly melted powder, solute undercooling). They break the competition and weaken the texture, which matches the near-random specimen reported at the highest speed by Ishimoto et al. (2020).

    Notation

    ⟨110⟩∥BD means a ⟨110⟩ crystal direction points along the build direction. {110}⟨001⟩ (Goss-type) adds the in-plane part: {110} planes face BD and ⟨001⟩ lies along the scan direction. In IPF colouring red is ⟨001⟩, green ⟨101⟩, blue ⟨111⟩. MRD = multiples of a random distribution.

    What the model does, and what it leaves out

    Model

    1. Melt pool size. Width from the Rosenthal moving point-source solution plus a beam-size term. Depth from a conduction aspect ratio of 0.38 that grows with the normalised enthalpy ΔH/hs once it passes the keyhole threshold πTb/Tm (Hann et al. 2011). Keyhole pools also get steeper walls.
    2. Cross-section. Each track is a U-shaped pool, boundary depth D(1 − |x/a|ⁿ), stacked at hatch spacing h and layer thickness t; later pools overwrite earlier ones. Inside each pool the heat flow is taken normal to the nested pool shape through that point.
    3. Grain competition. A line of sites across the hatch is built up layer by layer from a random baseplate. At each layer a site keeps its crystal or is overgrown by a neighbour whose ⟨100⟩ axes fit the local heat-flow directions better (Walton–Chalmers style selection). Overgrowing branches carry a small misorientation. Stray grains appear with a probability that rises with speed and with shallow remelting. For rotated layers, the same pool cross-section is applied along the rotated transverse direction.
    4. Texture. Orientations from the top 20 layers of four independent lines (about 15 000 orientations) feed the pole figure, inverse pole figure and family shares, each normalised against a random sample.

    Simplifications

    • Cells only feel the heat-flow component in the transverse–build plane. Real cells also tilt toward the scan direction at the back of the pool, especially at high speed.
    • Melt pool size is an analytic estimate with constant properties and absorptivity. Marangoni flow, vapour recoil and powder effects are ignored. Calibrate D and W against your own single-track cross-sections before trusting numbers.
    • Stray-grain probability is phenomenological, chosen to reproduce the trend, not derived.
    • No contour scans, island/stripe patterns, interlayer hatch offsets, gas-flow effects (Andreau et al. 2019), solid-state recrystallisation or δ-ferrite.
    • The 67° fibre axis reported for 316L varies between studies. The model predicts a mainly ⟨100⟩ fibre, as Sun et al. (2018) found in Ni–25Mo; treat it as a prediction to test.
    • Trends are the lesson. Absolute MRD values and process windows are illustrative.
