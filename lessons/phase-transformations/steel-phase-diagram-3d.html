2 · Reading it

What the surfaces are

Ae3

The temperature where ferrite first appears on slow cooling. Manganese pushes it down and silicon pushes it up. On the hypereutectoid side it becomes the line where ferrite joins austenite and carbide.

Acm and upper Ae1

The temperature where the first carbide appears. Below the eutectoid carbon it is the upper Ae1, the top of the three-phase band. Above it, it is Acm, the cementite solubility line.

Lower Ae1

The temperature where the last austenite disappears. In plain Fe–C the two Ae1 temperatures are one line at 726.5 °C. A third element opens them into a band where ferrite, austenite and carbide coexist.

Two features stand out. With manganese the lower Ae1 drops fast at low carbon and stops at a flat step at 481 °C, where TCFE14 has a four-phase reaction between austenite, ferrite, cementite and M₂₃C₆. With silicon the Ae3 at very low carbon climbs steeply and then leaves the block, because silicon closes the austenite loop.

3 · How it was computed

Data and method

  • Thermo-Calc with TCFE14, metastable Fe–Fe₃C (graphite suspended). Manganese from 0 to 6 at% and silicon from 0 to 5 at% in 0.25 at% steps, carbon from 0.01 to 1.5 wt% in 0.025 wt% steps.
  • Each surface point is a bisection on single equilibria to 0.5 °C, with ferrite, austenite and carbide told apart by phase and carbon content. The slice on the right is Thermo-Calc's own isopleth mapping of the same section.
  • The plain Fe–C row (0 at%) is read off the binary mapping. Near 1.5 wt% C the bisection could not find Acm because the hot end of its window was partly liquid, so those points come from the mapped cementite line instead. Nothing was smoothed, and the few points where a bisection failed are left as small gaps.
  • Everything is equilibrium. Below about 500 °C, substitutional diffusion is too slow for these temperatures to matter in practice.
