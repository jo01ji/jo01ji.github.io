How a third element moves Ae1, Ae3 and Acm

With a third element the single A1 temperature splits into a band where ferrite, austenite and carbide coexist (hatched). Its top is the upper Ae1, where the first carbide appears on cooling. 3 · Reference values

Key temperatures and compositions

Textbook ranges: Callister & Rethwisch (after ASM / Massalski 1990) and Okamoto, J. Phase Equilib. 13 (1992) 543. TCFE14 builds on Gustafson's 1985 Fe–C assessment, so it lands within about 1 °C of them.

Ae, Ac, ArA for arrêt, a thermal arrest. Ae is equilibrium, Ac is measured on heating, Ar on cooling. Ac > Ae > Ar, and the gap grows with heating or cooling rate.
A1, A3, AcmA1 is the eutectoid line (lower critical). A3 is the γ / (α+γ) boundary (upper critical). Acm is the γ / (γ+Fe₃C) boundary.
A2, A4, A0A2 is the Curie temperature of ferrite, 770 °C, not a phase change. A4 is δ ↔ γ. A0 is the Curie temperature of cementite, about 210 °C.
T0 and T0′Below T0, austenite can turn into ferrite of the same composition, with no carbon partitioning. T0′ adds 400 J/mol of stored energy to ferrite (Bhadeshia & Edmonds 1980), the usual limit for bainite.
Extrapolated Ae3The γ/(α+γ) line continued below A1 with cementite suppressed. It gives the carbon in austenite at a local-equilibrium α/γ interface, which sets the supersaturation for allotriomorphs and Widmanstätten plates.
Lever ruleIn a two-phase field the fraction of each phase follows from the tie line. Because the compositions here are in wt%, the fractions are mass fractions.
4 · How it was made

Method and sources

All lines come from Thermo-Calc 2026a with the TCFE14 database, run through TC-Python. The Fe–C lines use Thermo-Calc's phase-diagram mapping, filled in with a monotone cubic between mapped points (this matches direct bisection to 0.1 °C). The alloy panels use single equilibria bisected to 0.5 °C at 22 carbon contents. Graphite is suspended except in the stable diagram. All values are full equilibrium. With Mn, Si and other substitutional elements, real transformations often follow paraequilibrium instead, so treat the alloy lines as the upper bound.
